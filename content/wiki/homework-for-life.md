# Homework for Life

**Summary**: A daily habit of logging one story-worthy moment, designed to ensure you always have material for the PAST framework.

**Lenses**: [[storytelling]]

**Sources**: [Give me 18min & I'll improve your storytelling skills by 183%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YtkrIaONxu0), [How to Tell Stories Better Than 99% of People](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lghSjBl9yGM), [Speak Better Than 99% of People: A Practical Guide to Storytelling](https://www.shannonjenkins.co/)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-25

---

Homework for Life is a practice attributed to storyteller Matthew Dicks, referenced in the [[past-storytelling-technique]] source.

## The Practice

- **Daily:** At the end of each day, write down one story-worthy moment in a spreadsheet or journal
- **Identify:** Find the [[five-second-moment]] — the instant you fundamentally changed or realized something
- **Refine:** Practice zooming in on that moment using Place, Action, Speech, and Thoughts
- **Meaning (optional addition):** Write one line on what the moment *meant*. This isn't part of the original practice. It's a wiki suggestion based on Shannon Jenkins's point that a story needs a takeaway (see [[what-happened-vs-what-it-meant]]), and it gives each logged moment a ready-made [[cart-framework]] Takeaway.

## Why it works

Most people believe they don't have interesting stories. Homework for Life reveals the opposite: every day contains at least one moment of genuine change or surprise. The practice trains you to notice and capture these moments before they fade.

Over time, the habit builds a personal archive of story material — indexed by the moments that actually mattered, not just the objectively "big" events.

## Using the Archive

Shannon Jenkins's advice to **look inward** when choosing a story (a conversation you still remember, a customer who said something unexpected, a project that went sideways) is exactly the kind of material this habit collects. Her approach runs the other way, though: decide the message first, then search for a story that supports it. The two fit together: log moments daily, then pick from them once you know the point. See [[choosing-the-right-story]].
