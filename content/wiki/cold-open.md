# Cold Open

**Summary**: Drop the audience straight into a high-stakes thought or question instead of warming up with small talk or introductions.

**Lenses**: [[storytelling]], [[executive]]

**Sources**: [Why Trying to "Sound Smart" is Losing You the Room | Strategic Story Producer Rob Willis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JBIeii_Qrrg), [How to Tell Stories Better Than 99% of People](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lghSjBl9yGM)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-25

---

By the time a speaker finishes "Hi, I'm so glad to be here, let's look at the agenda," the audience has already started to mentally check out. The cold open skips this: start with the hook, the stakes, or the question that matters, and let context follow.

> [!conflict]- Disputed: PAST and the five-line story open by grounding the scene first
> Everyone agrees on cutting the warm-up, but the PAST source says the first sentence should state the where and when, and the five-line story opens with the Situation. See [[debate-how-to-open]].

## Why It Works

Attention is not guaranteed — see [[captive-audience]]. Every second spent on pleasantries is a second the audience can use to disengage. A cold open spends the audience's limited attention on the point of the presentation instead of on throat-clearing.

## Relationship to Other Frameworks in the Wiki

This is the live-delivery counterpart to two structural ideas already in the wiki:

- **[[bluf-method]]** — BLUF puts the conclusion first in the *structure* of an argument. Cold open is the same instinct applied to the *opening moment* of a live talk: lead with the point, not the setup.
- **PAST** ([[past-storytelling-technique]]) — as noted in [[scqa-framework]], PAST "starts in action, not in setup." The cold open states this same principle as a rule for meetings and pitches, not just personal stories.

## Kinds of Cold Open

Shannon Jenkins (see [[how-to-tell-stories-better]]) offers two more ways to open once the small talk is gone:

- **Cold open (Willis):** skip the preamble; start with the hook, the stakes, or the question.
- **Scene opening (Jenkins):** start inside a specific moment. *"Three weeks before launch, my biggest customer looked across the table at me and said, 'This is never going to work.'"* This is a cold open built from a scene.
- **Unexpected opening (Jenkins):** start as far as possible from what the audience expects. Asked about the death of Earl the dog, Ted Lasso opens with being attacked by a dog as a toddler. The gap forces the audience to work out the connection. See [[surprise]].

A cold open is not the same as giving away the answer. Willis's version opens with a high-stakes *thought or question*, which creates [[curiosity]] and [[tension]]. A BLUF-style opening leads with the *conclusion*. Whether to lead with the answer or hold it back is its own disagreement; see [[debate-answer-first-or-last]].
