# Cold Open

**Summary**: Drop the audience straight into a high-stakes thought or question instead of warming up with small talk or introductions.

**Sources**: [Why Trying to "Sound Smart" is Losing You the Room | Strategic Story Producer Rob Willis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JBIeii_Qrrg)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-10

---

By the time a speaker finishes "Hi, I'm so glad to be here, let's look at the agenda," the audience has already started to mentally check out. The cold open skips this: start with the hook, the stakes, or the question that matters, and let context follow.

## Why It Works

Attention is not guaranteed — see [[captive-audience]]. Every second spent on pleasantries is a second the audience can use to disengage. A cold open spends the audience's limited attention on the point of the presentation instead of on throat-clearing.

## Relationship to Other Frameworks in the Wiki

This is the live-delivery counterpart to two structural ideas already in the wiki:

- **[[bluf-method]]** — BLUF puts the conclusion first in the *structure* of an argument. Cold open is the same instinct applied to the *opening moment* of a live talk: lead with the point, not the setup.
- **PAST** ([[past-storytelling-technique]]) — as noted in [[scqa-framework]], PAST "starts in action, not in setup." The cold open states this same principle as a rule for meetings and pitches, not just personal stories.
