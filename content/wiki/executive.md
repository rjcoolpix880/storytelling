## What this lens is about

Clear, direct communication for busy listeners who need to understand, decide, or act. The listener's time and attention are the scarce resource, so the job is to make the point obvious, make the argument easy to follow, and keep people with you.

- **Lead with the point.** Bottom line first, then support ([[bluf-method]], [[minto-pyramid-principle]]); this clashes with the storytelling lens (see [[debate-answer-first-or-last]]).
- **Give the argument a shape.** [[scqa-framework]], [[what-so-what-now-what]], [[monroes-motivated-sequence]], and [[mece-framework]] structure the case so it can be scanned and repeated.
- **Show the gap.** Persuasion lives between the present and a better future ([[what-is-vs-what-could-be]]).
- **Earn attention the whole time.** No one is a [[captive-audience]]: open strong ([[cold-open]]), respect the [[18-minute-rule]], and favor discussion ([[three-to-one-ratio]]).
- **Stories in service of the point.** A short business story is chosen to back a message you've already decided ([[choosing-the-right-story]]) and shows the person behind the expertise ([[competence-and-warmth]]).
- **Presence by subtraction.** Authority comes as much from dropping habits as adding skills: hedged opinions, waiting to be asked, rambling ([[executive-presence]], [[strong-opinions-loosely-held]]).
- **Belief over polish.** Conviction and clarity persuade more than sounding smart ([[belief-vs-confidence]], [[sounding-smart]]).
