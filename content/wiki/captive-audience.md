# No Captive Audience

**Summary**: Treat every audience as one that could walk away at any moment — attention must be earned continuously, not assumed just because people are seated in the room.

**Sources**: [Why Trying to "Sound Smart" is Losing You the Room | Strategic Story Producer Rob Willis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JBIeii_Qrrg), [How to Tell Stories Better Than 99% of People](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lghSjBl9yGM)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-22

---

Rob Willis traces this lesson to giving free walking tours in Berlin: because nobody paid upfront, a boring guide simply lost the crowd — people voted with their feet.

## The Boardroom Trap

In a corporate meeting, the audience is physically stuck in their seats, so speakers get lazy — they coast on the assumption that people have to stay. Willis's rule: present as if the audience could walk out the door at any second, even when they technically can't.

## Attention Drift Is a Delivery Problem, Not a Content Problem

If people are visibly disengaging, the content is rarely the cause — the delivery is. This reframes attention loss as something the speaker can fix in the moment (energy, pacing, a hook) rather than something to blame on the subject matter.

Shannon Jenkins's lessons give story-level versions of these fixes: [[pacing]] (slow down for what matters, speed through the rest), [[curiosity]] (a question the audience wants answered), and [[tension]] (a reason to care what happens next). See [[how-to-tell-stories-better]].
