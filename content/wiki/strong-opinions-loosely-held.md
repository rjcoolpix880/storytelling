# Strong Opinions, Loosely Held

**Summary**: State your view with conviction and make the recommendation yourself, but stay open to changing it if someone makes a better case. The fix for burying a clear opinion under hedges.

**Lenses**: [[executive]]

**Sources**: [Speak Like a Leader](mailto:hello@shannonjenkins.co)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-25

---

Shannon Jenkins's fix for the first habit that undermines [[executive-presence]]: weak opinions (see [[speak-like-a-leader]]).

## The Problem: A Clear View Nobody Hears

Weak opinions take two forms:

1. **Hedging.** You have a view, but you wrap it in so many qualifiers that nobody hears it.
2. **Handing off the decision.** You lay out the options and leave someone else to choose.

Jenkins's list of hedges:

- "I'm not sure, but..."
- "This might be a silly question..."
- "I just think maybe we could..."
- "I could be wrong, but..."
- "Does that make sense?"

The cost: by the end of the conversation, you've given people little reason to trust your judgement.

## The Fix

- **Don't:** *"I just think maybe we could look at option B? I could be wrong, but... does that make sense?"* (The wiki's composite of Jenkins's hedges, not a quote.)
- **Do:** *"There are three options. I recommend B, and here's why."*
- **Why:** The strong version shows you've done the thinking (three options), makes a decision (B), and invites scrutiny on your terms (here's why).

## Strong *and* Loose

The two halves do different jobs:

| | Strong | Loosely held |
|---|---|---|
| **What it means** | State the view with conviction; make the call | Change it if someone makes a better case |
| **What it protects against** | Hedging, handing off the decision | Stubbornness, defending a bad call |
| **What it earns** | Trust in your judgement | Trust that you're listening |

"Loosely held" is what makes the strong opinion safe to state. You're not claiming certainty, you're making a recommendation you're prepared to revise.

## How This Relates to Other Pages

- **[[bluf-method]].** "I recommend B, and here's why" is BLUF in one sentence: the recommendation first, the reasons underneath. BLUF's "Ask" and Minto's bottom line (see [[minto-pyramid-principle]]) both assume the speaker has an opinion to lead with.
- **[[belief-vs-confidence]].** Rob Willis says audiences respond to genuine conviction. Strong opinions are that conviction applied to a recommendation. Note that Willis uses "confidence" to mean polish, not conviction, so his "belief, not confidence" doesn't argue against Jenkins.
- **[[story-killers]].** "Does that make sense?" is on both lists. The PAST source says it signals doubt and breaks a story's immersion; Jenkins says it signals doubt and undercuts your authority. "I'm not a good storyteller" on the PAST list is a pre-emptive apology, the same move as "This might be a silly question..."
- **[[what-so-what-now-what]].** A strong opinion is a clear *Now What*. Ending a status update with the facts and no recommendation is the handing-off-the-decision form of a weak opinion.
