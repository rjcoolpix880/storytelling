# Feel, Not Just Think

**Summary**: Facts and logic matter, but emotion is what makes people embrace an idea and act on it. When preparing, ask "What do I want them to feel?" as well as "What do I want them to think?"

**Lenses**: [[storytelling]], [[executive]]

**Sources**: [How to Sell Your Ideas Better Than 99% of Your Colleagues](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kYv9N8F7l5o), [Why Trying to "Sound Smart" is Losing You the Room | Strategic Story Producer Rob Willis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JBIeii_Qrrg)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-21

---

Shannon Jenkins calls this the most important of her five lessons from Don Draper's Kodak pitch (see [[how-to-sell-your-ideas]]): don't rely only on rational, logical arguments.

"Logic is not the key ingredient of a winning pitch."

## The Claim

Jenkins doesn't throw logic out: "Of course, facts matter. Logic matters." But if you want people "to genuinely embrace your idea, for it to resonate, and for them to have that full motivation to act, think about what you want them to feel, not just what you want them to think."

- **Don't (only ask):** *What do I want them to think?*
- **Do (also ask):** *What do I want them to feel?*

## Explaining vs. Making Them Feel It

The turning point of Don's pitch is when he stops explaining and starts making the room feel the idea:

> "Teddy told me that in Greek, nostalgia literally means the pain from an old wound. It's a twinge in your heart, far more powerful than memory alone."

Then his own family photos appear on the screen. Jenkins's reading: the audience *experiences* nostalgia instead of just understanding the definition of the word. His tools are evocative language ("pain from an old wound," "a twinge in your heart") and vulnerability (his own family). See [[competence-and-warmth]].

## Agreement Across Sources

This is one of the wiki's strongest points of agreement.

- **Rob Willis:** audiences "want to be led by a believer," and persuasion is connection, not data. His Knowledge Deficit Trap, adding information when people don't buy in, is the opposite of Don's move. See [[sounding-smart]].
- **Duarte's sparkline** measures what the audience felt (claps, laughs, wonder), not what was said. See [[duarte-resonate]].
- **The storytelling sources** (PAST, Van Neistat, Jenkins's Ted Lasso material) all aim at the listener experiencing a moment rather than being told about it. See [[five-second-moment]] and [[what-happened-vs-what-it-meant]].
- **[[truth-vs-facts]]:** emotional truth over factual precision. Don's pitch is an example: his definition of nostalgia isn't the literal Greek, and it doesn't matter.

**Not a conflict with logic-first structures (wiki note).** Minto's pyramid and Monroe's Need step lean on evidence and data. Jenkins explicitly says facts matter, so this is a different emphasis, not a rejection. A pitch can be logically structured and still aim at a feeling.

## Adding "Feel" to Preparation (wiki synthesis)

Jenkins's storytelling guide starts with: *What do I want my audience to understand, believe or remember?* (see [[choosing-the-right-story]]). This lesson adds a fourth word: **feel**. In the Kodak pitch, "nostalgia" is both the message and the feeling.
