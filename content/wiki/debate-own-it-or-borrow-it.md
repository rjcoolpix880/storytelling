# Debate: Own Your View, or Borrow Someone Else's Authority?

**Summary**: Shannon Jenkins's email says to state your recommendation as your own ("I recommend B, and here's why"), while her Don Draper video says not to open with "I think" and to credit an expert instead.

**Lenses**: [[executive]]

**Sources**: [Speak Like a Leader](mailto:hello@shannonjenkins.co), [How to Sell Your Ideas Better Than 99% of Your Colleagues](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kYv9N8F7l5o)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-21

**Type**: Different context

---

## The question

When you give a recommendation, should you put your own name on it, or present it as something you learned from someone else?

Both positions come from the same author and the same lens (executive). The sources don't comment on each other.

## Position 1: *Speak Like a Leader* — own the view and make the call

Weak opinions undercut your authority: hedges like "I'm not sure, but..." and "I could be wrong, but...", or laying out the options and leaving the decision to someone else. By the end of the conversation you've given people "little reason to trust your judgement." The fix is "strong opinions, loosely held": state your view with conviction, stay open to a better case. See [[strong-opinions-loosely-held]] and [[speak-like-a-leader]].

**Example:**
- **Don't:** Lay out the options and leave the decision to someone else.
- **Do:** *"There are three options. I recommend B, and here's why."*

## Position 2: *How to Sell Your Ideas* — don't make it you versus them

If you need to challenge somebody's thinking, saying "I think" makes it a contest between you and them. Bring in another perspective ("an expert, a customer, a mentor, a data point, a story") so people get curious rather than defensive. Don Draper presents his idea as something Teddy, an old pro copywriter, taught him. "Suddenly, Don isn't telling the client that they're wrong and he's right." See [[third-party-perspective]] and [[how-to-sell-your-ideas]].

**Example:**
- **Don't:** "I think we should do X."
- **Do:** "I learned from [Expert] that..."

## Where they agree

Both reject the hedge. Don's opinion is not weak: he is calm, measured, and clearly leading the client (see [[vocal-and-physical-authority]]). The disagreement is narrower than it looks. It's about *whose name* goes on a strong view, not *whether* to hold one.

## The wiki's view (optional)

*This is synthesis, not a verdict.*

- **Different jobs.** The email is about decisions and status updates, where people want your judgement. The video is about challenging a view the audience already holds, where the risk is defensiveness. Hence the label "different context."
- **They can combine.** A third party can open the door and your own recommendation can walk through it: "Teddy taught me X. So I recommend B."
- **A risk in each.** Owning every view can turn a disagreement into a contest. Always borrowing authority can read as not having a view of your own, the thing the email warns about.
