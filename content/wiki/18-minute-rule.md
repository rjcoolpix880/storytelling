# The 18-Minute Attention Span

**Summary**: Cognitive fatigue sets in around the 18-minute mark of any presentation — the reason TED Talks are capped there — so a talk must end, shift energy, or invite participation before that wall hits.

**Sources**: [Why Trying to "Sound Smart" is Losing You the Room | Strategic Story Producer Rob Willis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JBIeii_Qrrg)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-10

---

Attention is a finite resource. Past roughly 18 minutes, an audience cannot absorb more data without a break, a shift in energy, or active participation.

## The Practical Rule

Communication is not just about output — it's about whether the message is actually *received*. If given a 30-minute meeting, the worst move is to talk for all 30. The presentation should end well before the cognitive wall, leaving room to shift into dialogue.
