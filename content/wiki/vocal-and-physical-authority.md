# Vocal and Physical Authority

**Summary**: Delivery signals that convey authority: a slow, measured voice, sentences that end on a neutral tone instead of rising like a question, and hand movements kept in the "zone of authority" between shoulders and hips.

**Lenses**: [[executive]]

**Sources**: [How to Sell Your Ideas Better Than 99% of Your Colleagues](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kYv9N8F7l5o)

**Last updated**: 2026-10-21

---

Shannon Jenkins's first technique for introducing an idea (see [[how-to-sell-your-ideas]]): speak with authority and conviction. Her description of Don Draper:

> "His voice is slow and measured. He finishes his sentences on a neutral tone, never lifting his voice at the end as if he's asking for approval or permission. His body language communicates the same confidence. His movements are controlled and his hands stay mostly in what I call the zone of authority."

## The Voice

- **Slow and measured.** No rush to get through it.
- **End on a neutral tone.** Many people end statements with a rising inflection (uptalk), which makes a statement sound like a question or a request for validation. Finishing neutral, or slightly down, anchors it as a fact.
  - **Don't:** End a recommendation on a rising note, as if asking "Is that OK?"
  - **Do:** Land the last word level or slightly lower.
  - **Why:** Uptalk sounds like you're asking permission to be heard.

## The Body: The Zone of Authority

Keep your hands mostly between your shoulders and your hips. It avoids nervous, high-energy, or erratic movements, and signals that you are composed, in control, and worth trusting.

## Leading, Not Asking

Jenkins's reading of Don's pivot from "technology" to "sentiment": the measured tone and controlled gestures signal that he is leading the client, not asking for permission. That is the same thing Jenkins's email warns against in words (see [[speak-like-a-leader]]). Uptalk is the vocal version of "Sorry, can I just jump in..."
