# The Minto Pyramid Principle

**Summary**: Barbara Minto's top-down communication structure — bottom line first, then key points, then supporting data — designed for busy audiences who scan rather than read.

**Sources**: [The Minto Pyramid Principle Explained (With Example Frameworks)](https://www.betterup.com/blog/minto-pyramid), [How to Tell Stories Better Than 99% of People](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lghSjBl9yGM)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-22

---

Developed by Barbara Minto — the first female MBA at McKinsey — in the 1970s and codified in her 1985 book *The Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing and Thinking*. Still taught to new hires at McKinsey and most other major consulting firms.

The principle is structurally identical to [[bluf-method]] — Minto explicitly calls her top tier "answer-first communication" and references BLUF by name — but Minto adds two scaffolding frameworks: [[scqa-framework]] for the top, and [[mece-framework]] for the base.

## The Three Tiers

### 1. Bottom Line
State your overall argument, finding, or recommendation first. Minto structures this top tier using the [[scqa-framework]] — Situation, Complication, Question, Answer — so the bottom line arrives as a resolved mini-story rather than a bare claim.

### 2. Key Points
Summarize the main reasons supporting your bottom line. More content than the top tier, but still short enough to hold attention.

### 3. Details and Data
The content-heavy base. Show your work: data, case studies, research. Minto recommends organizing this section using the [[mece-framework]] — mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive categories.

## Alternative Orderings for Key Points

The bottom line must come first, but the key-points section can be organized in several ways depending on the audience:

- **Sequential** — chronological; helps the audience follow your thinking process
- **Importance** — most critical details first to build trust
- **Impact** — most emotionally weighted information first for memorability

## Worked Example — Gen Z Sales Strategy

The source uses a clothing brand investing in Gen Z marketing:

**Bottom line (SCQA-structured):** "Company X will increase revenue by $100 million over five years by investing $30 million in marketing to target the 11–26 age demographic, otherwise known as Gen Z. Gen Z currently accounts for one out of every five Americans. They have a total spending power of $360 billion, but Company X is seeing only 5% of its sales from this age bracket..."

**Key points:** Market information → Marketing strategy changes → Business impact (including the cost of losing ~2% of millennial/Gen X clientele)

**Details and data:** Market studies, customer profiles, year-over-year projections.

## Why It Works

Minto built this for executives with no time to read walls of text. The Nielsen Norman Group finding cited in the source — 79% of people scan web pages rather than read them — is the modern restatement of the same problem. Pyramid structure makes the scannable layer carry the argument.

Stating the conclusion first also frames every subsequent detail around it, which is more persuasive than letting the audience build their own interpretation as you go.

> [!conflict]- Disputed: Van Neistat and Jenkins say leading with the key fact ruins a story
> Van Neistat calls leading with the key fact the amateur's mistake; Shannon Jenkins says to withhold the payoff. Duarte's SCQA template and Monroe's sequence also build up before the answer. See [[debate-answer-first-or-last]].

## How It Connects to Other Frameworks

- Minto's top tier *is* [[bluf-method]] — same idea, different vocabulary. Minto's contribution is the structure underneath: SCQA on top, MECE underneath.
- The [[scqa-framework]] within Minto also appears in [[duarte-resonate]] as the story template. Two independent sources converge on the same four-beat structure for arguments.
- MECE is a [[useful-vs-complete]] tool — it forces categorization that is rigorous (complete) but also non-overlapping (useful for analysis).
- The "answer-first" insight here parallels [[jit-context]]: lead with what the listener needs first, hold the supporting context until requested.
- The SCQA top tier builds a small amount of [[tension]] (the Complication) and [[curiosity]] (the Question) around the answer.
