# What / So What / Now What

**Summary**: A three-beat framework for simplifying an argument before building a presentation — the facts (What), why they matter (So What), and the action they call for (Now What).

**Lenses**: [[executive]]

**Sources**: [Why Trying to "Sound Smart" is Losing You the Room | Strategic Story Producer Rob Willis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JBIeii_Qrrg), [How to Tell Stories Better Than 99% of People](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lghSjBl9yGM), [Speak Like a Leader](mailto:hello@shannonjenkins.co)

**Last updated**: 2026-09-25

---

Before building slides, Rob Willis recommends distilling the argument into a simple structure so it can be explained in two to three minutes. "What, So What, Now What" is one such structure: state the facts, explain why they matter, then say what to do about it.

## The Three Beats

1. **What** — the observation or fact on its own.
2. **So What** — why that fact matters to this audience, specifically.
3. **Now What** — the action or decision it calls for.

## Relationship to SCQA

Willis names a second option in the same breath: "Situation, Complication, Resolution" — which is [[scqa-framework]] with the Question step folded into the Complication rather than stated separately. What/So What/Now What is a different shape, not a restatement of SCQA:

| SCQA | What / So What / Now What |
|---|---|
| Situation + Complication | What (the fact/observation) |
| Question | So What (why it matters) |
| Answer | Now What (the action) |

The difference in emphasis: SCQA is built to justify a specific answer to a specific complication (argument-shaped). What/So What/Now What is more general-purpose — it works for status updates and reflections that don't necessarily hinge on a single complication, as long as they end in an action.

## Status Updates and Recommendations

Shannon Jenkins's example of rambling is "a status update that starts in 2023" (see [[speak-like-a-leader]]). The fix is to lead with the main point, which in this structure means the *So What* comes quickly, not after a long *What*. And a *Now What* that lays out options without choosing one is what Jenkins calls a weak opinion. Her alternative: *"There are three options. I recommend B, and here's why."* See [[strong-opinions-loosely-held]].

## Relationship to CART and "What It Meant"

Shannon Jenkins's [[cart-framework]] (Context, Action, Result, Takeaway) is the story-shaped cousin of this structure:

| What / So What / Now What | CART |
|---|---|
| What | Context + Action + Result |
| So What | Takeaway (what it meant) |
| Now What | Takeaway turned back to the audience's situation |

Jenkins's central point, that a story must say [[what-happened-vs-what-it-meant]], is the *So What* beat applied to storytelling. Both frameworks warn against the same failure: stopping at the facts and leaving the audience to work out why they matter.
